# NSOS Affiliate Partner Pilot Charter

**Status:** Design and research only. This charter does not create affiliates, public pages, referral links, tracking, contracts, payouts, tax records, email campaigns, or public growth claims.

## Strategic Position

An affiliate programme can give NSOS a measurable distribution channel through credible technology educators, creators, and school-operations communities. It should be treated as a **controlled experiment**, not evidence that the product will grow quickly or at a particular scale. The pilot must protect school and learner data, make commercial relationships visible, and give NSOS approval control over every partner, claim, reward, and payout.

## Proposed Pilot Outcome

The proposed outcome is evidence about whether approved partners can introduce eligible institution decision-makers to the existing NSOS product without misleading marketing, intrusive tracking, or unapproved financial commitments. The programme should begin with a deliberately small, time-bounded cohort after legal, commercial, privacy, and payment approvals.

| Pilot element | Recommended starting rule | Explicit non-goal |
|---|---|---|
| Partners | Individually approved technology educators or content creators whose audience includes institution decision-makers | Open self-service affiliate enrolment |
| Offer | Truthful product demonstration, approved educational content, and a reviewable referral destination | Promises of outcomes, earnings, accreditation, student results, or rapid growth |
| Attribution | One opaque, revocable partner identifier on a consent-notified referral route, with aggregate reporting | Fingerprinting, learner tracking, sensitive data collection, or cross-site behavioural profiling |
| Conversion event | A clearly defined commercial milestone approved by NSOS before any reward is owed | Automatic payment based on clicks, leads, or unverified accounts |
| Payout | Manual owner/finance approval after fraud, contract, tax, and provider checks | Automated payment, wallet transfer, or commission calculation in the first release |
| Duration | A time-bounded pilot with a documented review decision | An irreversible public growth programme |

## Minimum Governance Requirements

Every proposed partner should be reviewed before receiving a referral identifier or any non-public product material. The review record should identify the responsible NSOS owner, approved channels, permitted messages, disclosure requirement, territory, term, and removal path. NSOS must be able to pause or revoke a partner promptly if content is misleading, impersonates a school, mishandles data, or violates the approved agreement.

The pilot should calculate only the information needed to judge partner attribution: partner identifier, referral timestamp, approved destination, consent state, and high-level lifecycle state. It should not put learner records, guardian data, payment evidence, passwords, user prompts, or referral identifiers into public logs, AI prompts, or partner-facing analytics. Referral reporting must be aggregated and role-restricted.

## Fraud and Claim Controls

The programme must reject self-referrals, duplicate claims, impersonated school registrations, unexplained traffic spikes, compensated testimonials presented as independent, and rewards attached to school fees or learner admissions. Partners must use an approved disclosure and must not make statements about NSOS capacity, security, payments, support response times, integrations, accreditation, certifications, outcomes, or available features unless the statement is factually supported and approved.

## Approval Gates Before Any Launch

1. Approve the precise commercial model, eligibility rules, commission event, payout cap, and refund or cancellation handling.
2. Approve legal terms, privacy notice, marketing disclosures, tax treatment, payment provider, and partner agreement.
3. Approve a small named pilot cohort and each partner’s content and referral destination.
4. Build only the approved consent, attribution, review, audit, and manual-payout controls, with tenant and role isolation tests.
5. Review measured pilot evidence before considering a wider programme. No public performance claim may be made without independently supportable evidence.

## Current Decision

The recommended next step is **research and approval design**, not implementation. NSOS should not contact creators, issue referral links, collect affiliate data, publish marketing material, make payment commitments, or run a public launch until the research and the explicit approval gates above are complete.

## Initial Compliance Findings

The Nigeria Data Protection Commission states that recognised lawful bases for processing include consent, legal obligation, and contract, and its public guidance highlights data-subject rights and the need for privacy notices. The pilot should therefore keep referral attribution minimal, identify the relevant processing basis before launch, and make its privacy information accessible at the referral destination.[1]

For Facebook and Instagram, Meta defines branded content as content influenced by a business partner in exchange for value, including payment or free gifts. Its policy requires use of the branded-content tool where applicable, prior permission to tag the business partner, and necessary disclosures of the commercial nature of content. NSOS should require approved partners to use the applicable platform disclosure tools and plain-language disclosures in every compensated or commission-bearing post.[2]

These findings inform product and programme design only. They are not legal advice, an advertising-clearance decision, or authorisation to process partner data, contract with creators, or pay commissions. Before any launch, NSOS should obtain a Nigeria-qualified review of the final partner terms, disclosure templates, referral notice, tax treatment, and any applicable advertising-clearance requirements.
